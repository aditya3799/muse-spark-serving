# Balancing expert work across the eight ranks

The policy uses only router counts observed earlier in the same run. It exchanges entire expert slots while retaining 32 on each rank. One slot holds 28,311,556 bytes of packed weights and scales. The planner considers a bounded number of exchanges. Reinstallation completes before the model publishes a new expert lookup in its existing buffer. Our two-process Gloo test checked all six weight and scale fields after consecutive exchanges, unchanged captured addresses and rejection of divergent plans before any mutation. A real H200 collective and live SGLang scheduler integration remain outstanding. On synthetic held-out draws from the competition's route distributions, exchanging up to four slots gave a 1.93× median ratio in *busiest-rank 16-row tile counts* at 768 rows with stable, strongly skewed expert usage. Turning over the hot experts entirely reduced the median to 1.00×, and the worst example was 0.78×. These figures describe the work distribution. They omit communication, migration, kernel time and the rest of serving. [Synthetic sensitivity](screen.json) · [Official expert workload](https://openkernelchallenge.dev/problems/muse_moe_w4a16)

For a separate test we downloaded excerpts of two public GLM routing captures with 256 experts and top-eight routing. They contain accepted generated tokens, and serve here as a test of how well routing patterns persist. In both capture sets, four unconstrained slot exchanges gave a median work-count ratio of 1.00×. Selecting migration policies on GLM-5.3 and confirming on GLM-5.2 improved the total maximum-rank work count by 0.26% at 128 rows and 1.17% at 768 rows. Adding one possible spare expert per rank, selected on the first 2,048 tokens, increased this proxy gain to 5.55% at 768 rows on the second capture, before replica and routing costs. The complete model speedup remains unmeasured.
