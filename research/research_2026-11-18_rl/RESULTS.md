## Ongoing optimization search

The search continues after each benchmarking batch. The active target is 2× official serving speedup, with the user's permission required for a scored attempt. A new placement policy exchanges entire experts after observing earlier requests in the same run. It preserved the original model's routing lookup across two local distributed workers. On two public routing traces, stability-aware slot exchange yielded limited gains in estimated work. On one 128-row synthetic skewed fixture, the slowest of eight sequentially measured rank computations took 34.54 ms under original placement and 29.88 ms after four exchanges. This comparison excludes communication and migration. [Placement algorithm and evidence](placement/FINDINGS.md)

The grouped expert candidate eliminates a scatter after the second expert matrix multiplication. Two independent local trials gave **1.0241× and 1.0236×** for the rank-local feed-forward block. The second trial's 95% interval was 1.0220–1.0252×. Raw and final outputs matched. The corrected serving adapter passed the same output checks. ## Where the local parent spends time

## SSEngine transfer to the scored server

SSEngine's four-B200 result measures first-token completion of 400 closed-loop sessions. It returns one greedy token per request. Its 40.51 requests/s compared with 4.64 for vLLM DP4/EP4 used three KV tiers in the SSEngine arm and HBM for that vLLM configuration. The SGLang incumbent already uses a host cache and reports aggregate hit rate 0.92 at every load. Tier transfer times are unavailable in the judge receipt. [Upstream evaluation](https://github.com/SyFI-ServingStudio/SSEngine-GLM5.3-Flash/tree/e17de72b4e918da1adfb60854f0bcd04828821b7) · [Incumbent cache evidence](../research_2026-11-18_rl_experts/ssengine_graph_prep/cache_receipts.json)

Following SSEngine's graph-input staging led to a scoped fix for the DFlash worker. In nine CPU scenarios it deferred work to the target runner, removed ten redundant staging calls in ten ordinary CUDA graph dispatches, and retained the same ten full static-buffer fills and tensor contents. The affected ID/position copy ranges are 128 to 4,096 bytes per step across the four loads. [Source, validation and applicability](../research_2026-11-18_rl_experts/ssengine_graph_prep/FINDINGS.md)

A separate scheduler experiment uses the pinned single-node TP shared-memory queue to detect an empty arrival poll. Across two eight-process Gloo trials with one arrival per eight polls, the local CPU stage earned signed rewards of +25.22 and +34.13 and lower latency on both seeds. All-empty trials were noisy against their duplicate-parent arms. Every-poll-nonempty showed no reliable advantage. The scored startup confirms a per-step poll, while runtime shared-ring presence and the exposed H200 host time still need verification. [Measured protocol and controls](../research_2026-11-18_rl_experts/scheduler_poll/CONTROLLED.md)

On 280 CPU scheduling scenarios with one GPU shared between prefill and decode, SSEngine's shortest-remaining policy and SGLang's shortest-prefill policy each gave about 0.976× the FCFS completion throughput for the simulated 32-user burst. A five-second aging threshold brought the former to roughly 0.999×. The upstream 60-second setting yielded 0.989×. These are workload sensitivities, with arrivals constructed from the scored workload means. [Scheduler simulation](../research_2026-11-18_rl_prefill/scheduler_srpt/replay.json)
