# One resident weight representation across all 60 routed layers

The experiment stores each local expert only in packed CUTLASS order. The original 864 MiB/rank/layer of FP4 coefficients and group-16 E4M3 scales remains 864 MiB, for 50.625 GiB over 60 layers. `GroupedFFN(..., exclusive=True)` passes the resident tensors directly to its two grouped GEMMs. W13 and W2 execute without reading or writing a conversion slab in this path. The raw storage uses the same bit encoding the CPU operand proof covered. Hopper FFN arithmetic and performance still need the actual SM90a gate. [Resident fast path](group_runtime.py) [Group validation](GROUP_RUNTIME.md)

**Installation and teardown.** `install_layer` operates on the prepared inner `_humming_layer` after the outer loader has released the native checkpoint aliases. It validates both projection formats, derives canonical parameters from the actual prepared Humming tensors, and commits the replacements together after both conversions succeed. The one-time extra live weight residency is 864 MiB while converting one layer, and zero once local references to the previous parameters drop. Consecutive layers must install sequentially on one load stream. Pinned CUDA graph pointers from an already captured Humming layer require tearing down those graphs before replacing its tensors. Reloads must reconstruct from the checkpoint and then reinstall the canonical format. The alternative does not modify checkpoint files. [Installer](exclusive_pack.py)

**Native fallback.** Create one `NativeFallback` per sequential execution stream and share it across layers. Its 576 MiB workspace fits the larger W13 native layout. `view(layer, local_ids)` compacts active expert indices on device. A bounded persistent Triton grid visits only present experts, restores words/scales to their original positions, and hands the original dtype and shapes to the pinned Humming method. The upstream getter retrieves the native weight and scale by name immediately before each GEMM. Both split W13 calls finish before W2, so repeated light/heavy accesses reuse one restoration per projection. `HummingMethod` retains the parent's kernel, launch geometry and FP32 arithmetic. An all-remote batch executes the control kernels with zero native weight bytes restored. [Native fallback](exclusive_pack.py) [Original getter](../../swarm_2026-11-16/moe/humming_vendor/humming/layer.py) [Parent split](../combined/parent/muse_spark_humming_split.py)

The 576 MiB fallback storage plus at most 12 MiB extra grouped activation padding and about 1.13 MiB of 16K-row bookkeeping yields roughly 589.1 MiB above the two parent intermediate allocations for one active stream, before CUTLASS and allocator workspaces. Multiple simultaneous streams need independent fallback storage or explicit serialization. Different graph-bucket adapters may share the same stream's arena only when the parent also shares graph intermediate pools or one provisioned maximum-sized activation arena is sliced appropriately. Repeatedly allocating all intermediate buffers per layer or per bucket would invalidate this model-wide bound.

The route and copy costs depend on the number of **distinct active experts**, not just total routes. One active expert requires 18 MiB of W13 and 9 MiB of W2 native storage. Restoring it performs 54 MiB of logical reads/writes. With eight unique uniformly sampled global experts per row, expected traffic is 387.6 MiB at M=8, 1,102.4 MiB at M=32, 1,698.3 MiB at M=128 and 1,727.5 MiB at M=256. An actual distribution concentrated on one expert costs 54 MiB at any size. These are conditional byte counts, not observations of judge routes or timings. [Calculations](exclusive_costs.json)

**Direct small-row Humming loader.** The rank-local weights use two distinct native G2S paths. `G2SMemoryLoaderB::load_tma` accepts an encoded tensor map and delivers its tiles directly to native shared memory. The legacy loader copies contiguous native `int4` vectors via `legacy_load_2d`. Group-16 scales have a separate loader. Changing only row strides cannot undo the nibble and scale permutations. A simple direct native-word gather over a canonical row-major bank requires four byte gather instructions. For 32 contiguous native output words at K=4096, the four gathers touch 8 sectors each, 16 distinct sectors, versus 4 sectors for contiguous native words. Caching and lane shuffles could amortize this, but the traffic count does not establish latency. Replacing both loaders also requires modifying `g2s_pipeline` TMA byte expectations or forcing the two relevant stages to legacy loads, then verifying visibility and barriers. An embedded direct producer has since compiled for the ordinary non-mbarrier subset on both architectures. CPU execution of the exact C++ bodies matched the native tile bytes. The other configurations retain the selective restorer. The actual conversion, routing and persistent restoration kernels passed 1,382,400 reference/poison byte comparisons across two fresh CPU interpreter seeds. Tests included both mini-block orderings, every active slot changed across calls, an empty map, full and partial sets and original parameter names and data types. Native-size Triton compilation produced 12 SM86/SM90 programs across active-list construction, W13/W2, and both mini-block orderings. Local GPU qualification is available with `qualify_fallback.py --seed 31079` or `--seed 77161`; it compares graph-captured native raw live values and the complete finalizer.
