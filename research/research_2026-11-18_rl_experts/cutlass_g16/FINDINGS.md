# CUTLASS grouped FP4 on Hopper

NVIDIA CUTLASS revision cd9d8a7d612b6b9ad0f1b698925e31243e828629 supports grouped mixed-input Hopper GEMMs through a problem-shape array, operand and scale pointer arrays, stride arrays, and a persistent grouped scheduler. Identically shaped problems permit grouped K-axis scaling. Fixed-size route tiles can meet the identical-shape requirement and permit one grouped call per projection. The BF16 example 69 instantiates two's-complement INT4. Example 55 exposes an E2M1 type flag. Changing the template type addresses weight encoding only. [Grouped interface](https://github.com/NVIDIA/cutlass/blob/cd9d8a7d612b6b9ad0f1b698925e31243e828629/examples/69_hopper_mixed_dtype_grouped_gemm/README.md#L5-L14) [Grouped arguments](https://github.com/NVIDIA/cutlass/blob/cd9d8a7d612b6b9ad0f1b698925e31243e828629/examples/69_hopper_mixed_dtype_grouped_gemm/69_hopper_int4_bf16_grouped_gemm.cu#L559-L608) [Example 55 encoding](https://github.com/NVIDIA/cutlass/blob/cd9d8a7d612b6b9ad0f1b698925e31243e828629/examples/55_hopper_mixed_dtype_gemm/55_hopper_int4_bf16_gemm.cu#L125-L140)

**The generic scaled mainloop cannot express Muse group-16 on packed FP4.** It accepts chunk size K or an integer multiple of the CTA's K extent. For full K=4096, group 16 fails the default BF16 CTA K=64. The K-major shared-memory selector for packed E2M1 requires a multiple of its smallest 128-bit contiguous K atom, which holds 32 four-bit elements. CTA K=16 therefore fails the layout constraint. Skipping the host can_implement check does not change the physical kernel. The scale stage still indexes one stored scale group per CTA K iteration. [Scale check and producer lookup](https://github.com/NVIDIA/cutlass/blob/cd9d8a7d612b6b9ad0f1b698925e31243e828629/include/cutlass/gemm/collective/sm90_mma_array_tma_gmma_rs_warpspecialized_mixed_input.hpp#L592-L606) [K atom selector](https://github.com/NVIDIA/cutlass/blob/cd9d8a7d612b6b9ad0f1b698925e31243e828629/include/cutlass/gemm/collective/builders/sm90_common.inl#L245-L261) [Shared layout definitions](https://github.com/NVIDIA/cutlass/blob/cd9d8a7d612b6b9ad0f1b698925e31243e828629/include/cute/atom/mma_traits_sm90_gmma.hpp#L78-L99)

Selecting an E4M3 scale element with a BF16 destination makes the generic fragment multiply in the scale's precision before conversion. Across all 4,064 finite nibble/scale pairs, a round-to-nearest-even, saturate-finite E4M3 emulation differed from the frozen Humming BF16 operand for 1,100 pairs after accounting for the fourfold parent exponent normalization. For example, FP4 6 × E4M3 0.171875 gives frozen normalized operand 0.2578125 versus 0.25 under FP8 staging. Supplying BF16 scale elements can fix this precision issue while leaving the group indexing problem. Expanding both W13 and W2 scale banks from one byte to two costs another 96 MiB per routed layer, or 5.625 GiB across 60 layers. A prior native W2 BF16-scale experiment also slowed the matched local FFN. [CUTLASS mixed-precision scale operation](https://github.com/NVIDIA/cutlass/blob/cd9d8a7d612b6b9ad0f1b698925e31243e828629/include/cutlass/detail/collective/mixed_input_utils.hpp#L921-L948) [CPU counterexample and all pairs](screen.json) [Native Humming scale test](../scale_expand/FINDINGS.md)

A concrete custom mainloop could pack the four existing E4M3 bytes for a 64-wide K tile into one uint32 in channel-major storage. Every 16-wide BF16 GMMA then selects one byte by its K-block index, decodes it into BF16 registers, and multiplies with the FP4 fragment using the frozen quarter-weight normalization. The exact scale bits, signed values and BF16 parent operands matched in 65,024 CPU comparisons covering all finite scale encodings through all four selectors. This preserves scale byte count. Per-output-row integral restoration after GEMM must retain the existing BF16 rounding followed by the parent factor of four. The custom mainloop and WGMMA reduction ordering have no hardware or compile qualification. [Executable scale-word proof](probe.py) [Packed scale result](screen.json)

A fixed 16-route descriptor for every occupied expert tile would pad at most 32 × 15 = 480 local route rows per GPU. It must use a route-count scan and stable gather/scatter or equivalent indexing to avoid host synchronization. The 16,384-row prefill fixture typically gives roughly 16,384 local routes under uniform EP8 routing, but hidden serving balance is unknown. Packing a second complete W13/W2 weight bank into the example's required layout would cost 768 MiB of FP4 weights plus 96 MiB of scale storage per routed layer. Three dual-resident layers would exceed the total two-GB allowable build-over-stock delta before any scratch. Real deployment would require one common packed layout also readable by the retained decode Humming kernel, or measured, bounded runtime repacking. Copying the example's shuffled representation alongside Humming is ineligible. [CUTLASS layout and shuffle](https://github.com/NVIDIA/cutlass/blob/cd9d8a7d612b6b9ad0f1b698925e31243e828629/examples/69_hopper_mixed_dtype_grouped_gemm/69_hopper_int4_bf16_grouped_gemm.cu#L104-L131) [Arithmetic and memory](screen.json)

The earlier Hopper serving port used 43 expert launches per eligible 16,384-row layer, passed accuracy and was excluded on all loads. C16 raw throughput fell from 15,546.7 on the proven parent to 6,450.1 tokens/s/GPU. No per-kernel H200 timing attributed that regression to launch overhead. The proposed grouped-launch mechanism is distinct, but the group-16, FP4-layout, Humming accumulator and residency requirements block a drop-in experiment. No measured gain or score credit is assigned.
